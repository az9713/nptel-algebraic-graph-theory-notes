Graph surgery laboratory

Change one edge and compare connectivity, tree redundancy and the Laplacian spectrum.

The model is a finite undirected simple graph on the fixed vertex set $V=\{0,1,2,3,4\}$. Every enabled edge is unweighted. The drawing records incidence only: its geometric lengths have no physical meaning. The initial square, diagonal and leaf are the recurring original teaching construction.

What the quantities measure

The adjacency matrix $A$ has entry $A_{ij}=1$ exactly when $i$ and $j$ are joined. Its diagonal is zero. The degree $d_i=\sum_j A_{ij}$ counts neighbors. The diagonal degree matrix is $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_0,\ldots,d_4)$, and the combinatorial Laplacian is $L=D-A$.

A connected component is a maximal vertex set in which every pair can be joined by a path. The cycle rank is $m-n+c$, where $m$ is the edge count, $n=5$ and $c$ is the component count. It counts independent cycle directions, rather than the total number of simple cycles. The matrix-tree theorem counts spanning trees by a principal cofactor of $L$. A disconnected graph has zero spanning trees on the full vertex set.

The displayed Laplacian eigenvalues are ordered increasingly. Their first value is zero up to floating-point rounding. The second eigenvalue is positive exactly when the graph is connected. As proved in §14.6, adding an edge adds a positive semidefinite rank-one matrix, so each ordered Laplacian eigenvalue cannot decrease. This statement does not say that every eigenvalue increases strictly.

For the initial graph, the spectrum is $0,1,2,4,5$ and every principal cofactor equals $8$. The product of the four nonzero eigenvalues divided by $5$ is $8$, providing a separate consistency check of the tree count. Remove the leaf edge $\{2,4\}$: the full graph disconnects and the tree count becomes zero. Restore it and remove the diagonal $\{0,2\}$: the count becomes four, the four choices of which square edge to omit.

Numerical scope

The browser uses Gaussian elimination for the cofactor and a Jacobi rotation eigensolver for a symmetric $5\times5$ matrix. Tree counts are rounded only after evaluating the determinant. This small laboratory is a teaching implementation, not a scalable graph solver. Its results are checked against exact integer determinants and exhaustive enumeration by the executable Python laboratory.
